- [[evaluation-of-vehicle-substances-on-vitamin-d-bioavailability-a-systematic-revie]] — Evaluation of vehicle substances on vitamin D bioavailability: A systematic review - PMC — Primary systematic review (Grossmann & Tangpricha, Mol Nutr Food Res, 2010; PMC3033429) examining how vehicle substances affect Vitamin D bioavailability. Identified 4 clinical studies comparing 2+ delivery vehicles. Key quantitative findings: oil-based vehicles produced the greatest mean rise in 25(OH)D per 100 IU/day (4.05 nmol/L per 100 IU/day), followed by powder-based vehicles (2.75 nmol/L per 100 IU/day), and ethanol (0.5 nmol/L per 100 IU/day). Most striking comparison: Maalouf et al. found oil vehicle produced 4.25x greater 25(OH)D increase than ethanol vehicle at 2000 IU average daily dose. Oil vs cellulose/lactose powder: mixed results — fish oil was 1.13x better than multivitamin tablet cellulose (not significant); lactose was 1.09x better than oil D2 (not significant). Mechanistic explanation: D3 is lipid-soluble, absorbed via micellar transport; fat in duodenum stimulates bile acid release which is required for D3 absorption. Oil vehicles facilitate integration into micelles. Conclusion: Oil-soluble vehicles produced greatest bioavailability but only one comparison (oil vs ethanol) reached statistical significance. Study notes fat malabsorption populations may have different vehicle responses. This is the key primary citation underlying most secondary source claims about oil/softgel superiority. `review`
- [[vitamin-d3-loaded-nanostructured-lipid-carriers-as-a-potential-approach-for-fort]] — Vitamin D3-Loaded Nanostructured Lipid Carriers as a Potential Approach for Fortifying Food Beverages; in Vitro and in Vivo Evaluation - PMC — Peer-reviewed study (Adv Pharm Bull, 2017) on Vitamin D3-loaded Nanostructured Lipid Carriers (NLC) for food beverage fortification. Key finding: incorporation of D3 into NLC (optimized formulation F3: Precirol solid lipid, Miglyol oil, 3% Poloxamer407, particle size ~87nm) significantly enhanced intestinal absorption versus conventional oily solution. In vivo rat gavage (2500 IU, n=6): NLC produced faster appearance of D3 in plasma and more prolonged plasma levels than Miglyol oil alone. Mechanism: NLC nano-range particles cross intestinal permeability barriers via Peyer's patches and M-cells, triggering lymphatic transport that bypasses first-pass liver metabolism. Precirol-based NLC showed superior stability (stable over 60 days) versus Compritol-based. Octyloctanoate introduced as first-use GRAS food-grade substitute for Miglyol oil in NLC preparation. Confirms that lipid-based carrier is essential for D3 bioavailability — directly relevant to why oil-filled softgels outperform dry tablets. `review`
