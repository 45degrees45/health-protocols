- [[an-assessment-of-vitamin-supplements-in-the-indian-market-pmc]] — An Assessment of Vitamin Supplements in the Indian Market - PMC — Peer-reviewed study (Indian J Pharm Sci, 2012; PMC3660877) by Chugh and Lhamo, Maulana Azad Medical College, New Delhi, surveying all vitamin supplements in Indian market using Drug Today 2010 compendium. Key findings from 936 total vitamin formulations: Most common single-ingredient product is Vitamin D3 (n=150; 16.02%), followed by folic acid (n=45; 4.80%). Combinations dominate: 76.49% of all products (n=716). Most common formulation is capsules (34.1%). Large number of preparations (Group II, n=40 single + more in combos) do not specify the amount of vitamins. Majority of supplements contain nutrient amounts higher than RDA. Authors call for urgent regulatory reform, better labeling requirements, and stricter enforcement for Indian supplement manufacturers. This is the companion study to PMC4852574 (which specifically analyzed Vitamin D formulations in 2013), providing broader market context. `review`
- [[analysis-of-select-dietary-supplement-products-marketed-to-support-or-boost-the]] — Analysis of Select Dietary Supplement Products Marketed to Support or Boost the Immune System — JAMA Network Open peer-reviewed study (August 2022, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.26040, PMC9366544) by Uniformed Services University researchers. Case series analysis of 30 immune health dietary supplements purchased from Amazon.com in May 2021. Key findings: 17 of 30 (57%) had inaccurate labels. Of those 17: 13 were misbranded (labeled ingredients not detected in product), 9 had unlisted ingredients detected. Only 13 of 30 (43.3%) had accurate labels. Zero of 30 products had a recognized third-party certification seal (NSF, USP, BSCG, Informed Sport). Products were top-featured items (4+ stars) in Amazon's immune health category. Price range: 1.93-0.48 per 30-day supply (median 5.33). Vitamin D was listed in 15 of the 30 products and zinc in 25; however, vitamin D3 specifically could NOT be verified analytically (method not sensitive for fat-soluble vitamins). Misbranded ingredients were mainly plant extracts. Of 30 tested products: 13 scored 4+ on OPSS Scorecard (less risky), 17 scored below 4 (risky). Critical methodological note: the analytical method did NOT test vitamin D3, so the mislabeling rate for botanical/herbal ingredients may not directly apply to single-ingredient vitamin D products. `review`
- [[is-it-safe-to-buy-supplements-on-amazon-fullscript]] — Is It Safe to Buy Supplements on Amazon? | Fullscript — Fullscript analysis (May 2024, updated Sep 2026) on supplement safety risks when purchasing via Amazon. Key findings: eCommerce represents 15% of total supplement market, with 77% of those supplement sales on Amazon. In a 2021 study, only 13 of 30 dietary supplements purchased on Amazon had accurate labels; 13 had ingredients listed that were not in the product; 9 had unlisted ingredients. None of the 30 products had third-party certification seals. Documents multiple specific counterfeiting incidents: NOW Foods (11 counterfeit products including psyllium husk, magnesium citrate, men's multivitamin), Fungi Perfecti (23 sellers of counterfeit Host Defense mushroom products), Regenexx, Pure Encapsulations, and Nature M.D. (man arrested in 2023 for selling counterfeits). Notes that sellers can send legitimate products for Amazon's required third-party testing while continuing to sell counterfeits. Amazon as of April 8, 2024 approves Eurofins, NSF International, and UL Solutions as third-party verifiers for supplements in sexual enhancement, weight management, and sports categories. Recommends checking for third-party certification seals, avoiding unknown brands, and using the OPSS Scorecard tool. `review`
- [[vitamin-d-supplements-in-the-indian-market-pmc]] — Vitamin D Supplements in the Indian Market - PMC — Peer-reviewed study (Indian J Pharm Sci, 2016; PMC4852574) by Lhamo, Chugh, Tripathi from Vardhaman Mahavir Medical College, New Delhi, systematically analyzing all vitamin D formulations available in the Indian market using Drug Today 2013 compendium. Key findings: 258 total vitamin D formulations available in India. Breakdown by active form: calcitriol 46.5% (n=120), alfacalcidiol 43% (n=111), cholecalciferol only 10.5% (n=27). Approximately 70-90% of apparently healthy Indian population is vitamin D deficient. Most common formulations are tablets (51.1%) and capsules (35.2%). Only cholecalciferol is the preferred form for prophylaxis/treatment of deficiency, but constitutes only 10% of market. High sales of calcitriol-containing calcium supplements risk hypercalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, nephrocalcinosis. Cost for VDD treatment: initial 8 weeks at INR 160, maintenance for 1 year at INR 240. IOM recommends up to 4,000 IU/day as safe; ICMR recommends 400 IU/day minimum sun exposure situations. Article calls for stricter regulation and labeling of vitamin D supplements in India. `review`
