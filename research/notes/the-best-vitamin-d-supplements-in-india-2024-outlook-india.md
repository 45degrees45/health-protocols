Factors to consider when shopping for a vitamin D supplement/tablet
Dosage:
Consider the dosage strength of the supplement. Dosages typically range from 400 IU (International Units) to 5000 IU per tablet. The appropriate dosage depends on factors such as age, health status, and existing Vitamin D levels.
Form:
Vitamin D supplements are available in various forms, including tablets, capsules, softgels, and liquid drops. Choose a form that is convenient and easy for you to incorporate into your daily routine.
Type of Vitamin D:
There are two main types of Vitamin D supplements: Vitamin D2 (ergocalciferol) and Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol). Vitamin D3 is typically recommended as it is more effective at raising blood levels of Vitamin D.
Quality and Purity:
Look for supplements from reputable brands or manufacturers known for their quality and purity standards. Consider supplements that have been tested by third-party organisations for potency and contaminants.
Additional Ingredients:
Check the ingredient list for any additional substances included in the supplement. Some supplements may contain additives, fillers, or allergens that you may want to avoid.
Bioavailability:
Consider the bioavailability of the supplement, which refers to the amount of Vitamin D that is absorbed and utilised by the body. Supplements with enhanced bioavailability may be more effective at raising Vitamin D levels.
Certifications:
Look for supplements that carry certifications from regulatory bodies or organisations, such as the United States Pharmacopeia (USP) or NSF International, which ensure that the product meets certain quality and safety standards.

Frequently Asked Question (FAQs)
How do I take Vitamin D supplements?
Vitamin D supplements are commonly available in various forms such as capsules, tablets, softgels, or liquid drops. Follow the dosage instructions provided on the product label or as advised by your healthcare professional, typically taken with a meal to enhance absorption.
Are Vitamin D supplements safe?
When taken as directed, Vitamin D supplements are generally safe for most individuals. However, excessive intake can lead to Vitamin D toxicity, characterised by symptoms like nausea, vomiting, and weakness. It's important to adhere to recommended dosage guidelines.
Can I get Vitamin D from food alone?
While some foods contain Vitamin D, it's often difficult to obtain sufficient levels through diet alone. Foods rich in Vitamin D include fatty fish, fortified dairy products, and egg yolks. Vitamin D supplements can help bridge the gap between dietary intake and optimal levels.
When should I take Vitamin D supplements?
Vitamin D supplements can be taken at any time of the day, preferably with a meal for better absorption. Consistency in daily intake is key to maintaining stable Vitamin D levels in the body.
How long does it take for Vitamin D supplements to work?
The time it takes to notice the effects of Vitamin D supplements can vary depending on individual factors such as baseline Vitamin D levels, dosage, and overall health. It may take weeks to months of regular supplementation to achieve optimal Vitamin D status.
